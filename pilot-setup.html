Pilot Setup

Our side of a 30-day pilot, from yes to the day 30 review. The restaurant gets the two-page pilot handout; this is the work behind it.

The terms (what we told them)

30 days, free, no card. We build their training from their own material and help everyone join at a pre-shift. We ask for that pre-shift, a feedback chat at the end, and permission to quote them if it goes well (they approve the exact words). After 30 days nothing renews or charges; price and next steps get settled at the review. List price is $79 a month per location.

Their copy of the handout

Add their name and start date to the link, then print it or text it:

seasoned.training/pilot.html?r=Prohibition&start=2026-10-14

The date is year-month-day. Day 30 and the weekdays fill in by themselves. Page 1 is for the owner; page 2 is the pre-shift script and the manager's day-one checklist.

Before day one

  • Justin Backups running and checked, before any real restaurant's data goes in.
  • Us Pick day one: a pre-shift when most of the team is there.
  • Them Their manager signs up at seasoned.training (Start your free trial) with their own email and names the restaurant the way staff say it.
  • Justin Add our manager accounts to their restaurant so we can build the packs, and switch it to active so the 14-day trial never pauses them mid-pilot.
  • Justin If more than about 25 staff will join on the restaurant's wifi in one hour, raise the sign-up limit first (it's per internet connection). Otherwise late joiners get told to switch to cell data.
  • Us Collect the material: photos of the binder pages, cocktail specs, the menu (PDF or link), the wine list, their steps of service if they have them, and a table map.
  • Us Build one pack per station (bar, kitchen, floor, wine). Import, then check every card against the source before publishing. Each manager account gets 40 imports a day, so a big binder goes over two days or across both our accounts.
  • Them Their chef reads every allergen line and signs off. Plain lines work best: "Gluten (flour), Dairy (butter)" or "None known." The 86 It drill appears once four or more dishes have allergen lines.
  • Us A Spanish copy of any pack the kitchen wants in Spanish, read over before publishing.
  • Us Publish. Print two posters (break room, time clock). Hand the manager the handout with page 2 on top.
  • Them First Tonight board and a first assignment ("Level 1 of your station by Sunday") posted, so day one isn't empty.

Day one

  • The manager sends the link to the staff group chat before pre-shift (Setup tab, Send the link).
  • The manager reads the script on page 2. Everyone scans and types a first name.
  • Watch the Players tab fill up. Fix any typo'd names with Rename, and split duplicate names ("Mike B.").
  • Ask the manager to pick one or two trusted servers to call 86s from the floor.
  • Stay for the first service if you can. The first question someone asks is the one the rest are thinking.

The month

  • Justin Once a week, look at who joined, who played, and how often Tonight got posted. Our own bar for "it's working": in some week, five or more staff each finish three levels, and the manager posts Tonight three times. That's ours to watch, not something we ask of them.
  • Us Anything they hit gets fixed, usually within a day. Write each one down; they're the review's best material.
  • Us Around day 15, a text to the manager: "Anything getting in the way?"
  • Us If they change the menu during the month, help them load it. That's the subscription pitch happening live.

The day 30 review

Sit down with the owner or GM (and the manager who ran it). Ask, then mostly listen:

  1. What did your staff actually use: the board, Lookup, the training?
  2. Did anyone get noticeably sharper? Who, and on what?
  3. What got in the way, or annoyed people?
  4. What's missing that would make it a must-have?
  5. Would you keep it at $79 a month? If not, what would make it worth it?
  6. Would you be OK with us quoting you? (Draft the line with them; nothing goes out until they approve it.)

After

  • Keep going Justin sets up billing once it exists. If we've moved to the production database by then, their restaurant moves with them.
  • Stop Thank them, and delete their restaurant's data if they ask (Justin).
  • Either way Send Lisa the approved quote, and write down what we learned for the next pilot.
