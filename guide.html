Manager Guide

Everything the restaurant might ask you about the app, on one page. Two minutes to read, keep it on your phone.

First: get yourself a sandbox

Best way to answer questions is to have the app open yourself. Go to seasoned.training (tap Manager sign-in), tap "New here? Set up your restaurant", make an account with your email, and name a practice restaurant (call it anything, "Jimmy Test" works). Now you have your own copy to poke at without touching theirs.

The two sides of the app

Staff side. No accounts. They enter the restaurant's join code and a first name, and they're training. The code and a scannable QR live on the manager's Setup tab.

Manager side. Email and password. "Manager sign-in" link sits at the bottom of the app's screens. Managers see the whole team's scores and edit all content.

The dashboard, tab by tab

  • Tonight: the daily board. Tonight's specials, what's 86'd, and a note to staff. The fastest form in the app; thirty seconds before service. The Share 86 list button drops the whole board into the staff group chat in one tap, for the phones that won't open the app.
  • Content (opens first): the training packs. This is where editing happens.
  • Players: every staff member who has trained, with best, average, and session counts. Tap a name for their history.
  • Recent: the latest completed levels, newest first.
  • Setup: who's signed in, the plan status, and the staff join code with its printable QR.

Editing content

Packs are quiz decks. Each carries a Live or Draft chip. Staff only ever see Live packs, so build quietly in Draft and publish when ready. You can unpublish any time; nothing is deleted.

  • New pack: start blank, or "New from template" to clone a starter. Besides the cocktail and kitchen samples there's an industry basics library (Wine 101, Beer Styles, Espresso Drinks) and Steps of Service for the front of house: ten universal procedures (the greet, running food, wine service, handling a complaint...) whose quiz asks "what comes next?" Clone it and rewrite the steps the house way.
  • Pack editor: title, icon, tagline, plus each level's name and lives. The six quiz mechanics themselves are fixed.
  • The small heading above the title is the station. Packs with the same heading (Bar Exam, Line Check, Industry Basics...) group together on the staff's Pick Your Station page. Make up any station name you like; the page organizes itself.
  • Items: each recipe is an item. Tap to edit, arrows to reorder, ✕ to delete. An item needs a name and at least one ingredient with both an amount and a name.
  • Publishing an empty pack is blocked on purpose. Add at least one item first.

Getting a menu in (show this off)

Nobody should type recipes. In any pack there are three import buttons; all of them land in a review screen where you untick anything that came out wrong before it saves.

  • 📷 Add from photos. Photograph recipe cards, binder pages, even neat handwriting. Select a whole stack of photos at once and review them as a batch. This works for the service manual too (each procedure becomes a numbered-steps card and the pack becomes a Floor station automatically) and for a floor plan (each table becomes a card with its seats and section).
  • 📄 Import PDF / CSV. Upload a menu PDF or a spreadsheet export. Excel files need a Save As CSV first.
  • 🔗 Import from menu URL. Paste a link to their menu page and the app reads it. If a site draws its menu with scripts the app will say so; use the menu's PDF link or photos instead. One of the three always works.
  • Always review before publishing. Compare against the original.
  • Allergens especially. The app only copies allergens that are literally written on the source. It never guesses them, so if the card doesn't list allergens, the item won't have them. A person has to verify that information.
  • Level names set themselves. Import a food menu and the levels take kitchen names (Mise en Place, Portion Control...); a drink menu gets the bar names (House Pour, Measure Up...). If you've renamed levels yourself the app leaves them alone.

The 86 It safety drill

Once at least four dishes have an Allergens section on their card, a third tile appears for staff automatically: ⛔ 86 It, an allergy fire drill. "Guest at table 12 has a fish allergy, which dish gets 86'd?" Wrong answers quote the real card, so it teaches. This is the owner pitch: games sell to staff, safety sells to owners. To light it up for a restaurant, make sure their dish cards carry allergen lines (verified by their chef, not by us).

The daily loop (this is the retention pitch)

Training is for week one. These three make the app something staff open every shift, and they run on the Tonight board:

  • The Tonight board. Manager posts specials, 86'd items, and a note. Staff see it as a card at the top of their screen when they open the app. Specials and the note expire after 24 hours on purpose (yesterday's board must never mislead tonight). The 86 list stays until the manager clears it.
  • Tonight's quick check. Post the board and a 3-question mini-quiz appears for staff automatically: each special with a one-liner becomes a question, and if something's 86'd, the last question is "which of these do you NOT sell tonight?" Managers see who did it under Recent.
  • The Daily One. One question every time a staff member opens the app, ten seconds, and it picks the thing that person got wrong most recently. There's a streak counter. A wrong answer keeps the streak; showing up is the habit, being right is the score.

POS sync (Toast and Square, more later)

On the Setup tab there's a "POS 86 sync" card. Connect the restaurant's Toast or Square and the 86 list fills itself the moment something is 86'd at the register. What to know when selling it:

  • Toast needs the right plan. Instant sync requires Restaurant Management Suite Essentials or higher (their standard API access). If the restaurant isn't on it, no problem: the 86 list works with manual entry today, and our Toast partnership will remove the plan requirement later. Ask which plan they're on; their Toast rep can quote the add-on.
  • Square works on any Square account. The manager copies an access token and webhook key from Square's developer dashboard; the card walks them through it.
  • The setup is a paste. The card gives them one URL to paste into their POS and step-by-step instructions. Synced items show a 🔌 marker on the board.
  • First connection at any restaurant: loop Justin in. The last mile of every POS integration gets finished against a real account.

Trying the quizzes yourself

From the dashboard, tap the ← arrow (top left). You get the staff menu with every Live pack, playable as a manager preview: your plays are never recorded, drafts stay hidden, and the footer link takes you back to the dashboard. Great for checking a pack before publishing, or for handing a prospect your phone at the table.

Quick answers to likely questions

"My staff sees 'code didn't match'"

Typo, almost always. The exact code is on the Setup tab. Easier: have them scan the QR there instead, it fills the code in.

"Staff can't see the new pack"

It's probably still Draft. Publish it, then have them close and reopen the app.

"It says training is paused"

That's the account plan, not a bug. Text Justin, he flips a switch.

"The app says offline"

It keeps working from saved content and uploads scores when signal returns. Nothing is lost.

"A bartender's stars disappeared"

Stars save per device. New phone means a fresh ladder. Their score history on the manager side is unaffected.

"I forgot my manager password"

Tap "Forgot password?" on the sign-in screen. A reset link lands in their email; have them open it on the phone they'll manage from.

"Where's the 86 It tile?"

It builds itself only when at least four dishes have an Allergens section on their cards. Add allergen lines (chef-verified) and it appears.

"Yesterday's specials vanished from the board"

By design. Specials and the note expire 24 hours after posting so nobody sells last night's fish. Post today's board; the 86 list carried over.

"The Daily One keeps asking the same dish"

Also by design. It picks what that person got wrong most recently and keeps asking until they get it right a couple of times. That's the point.

"A bartender wants to look something up mid-shift"

The search box at the top of their screen. Type two letters, get the full card. Works offline.

"The menu link import found nothing"

Some menu sites draw everything with scripts, so there's no text to read. Use the menu's PDF link or snap photos; those always work.

When to hand it to Justin

Anything about plans, billing, deleting a whole restaurant, account resets, or the app misbehaving in a way this page doesn't cover. You own "how do I use it", Justin owns "something's wrong with it".
